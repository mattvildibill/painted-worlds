# Painting-by-painting reconstruction review

The six rectified original canvases were inspected directly. The earlier world builders were compared against their placement, subject and material choices. The new entrance views were then compared to the paintings using depth-rasterized exports of the actual scene meshes, followed by an offset viewpoint to expose occlusion and edge problems. These checks do not constitute live browser/GPU testing.

| World / source | Largest previous mismatches | Reconstruction changes | Remaining limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lily Lake / 3177 | Circular lake with an invented jetty and boat; oversized nearby pines; generic separated mountain cones; weak broad watercolor reflections. | Broad foreground lake, ochre shoreline, low snowy central saddle and asymmetric dark mountain flanks traced independently. Sloped contact edges follow the right shoreline. Original water washes, paper texture, snow strokes and painted light are retained. Shore walking replaces the invented jetty route. | Shore and mountain distances are inferred. The lake retains the painted reflection rather than a physically recomputed reflection; reflected subjects therefore do not change with the camera as real reflections would. |
| Rose-colored peaks / 6343 | Peak scale and silhouette were too small/rounded; lake framing and tree placement were generic; added jetty distracted from the composition. | The main peak now towers beyond the upper canvas. Separate violet foothills, emerald lake, left larch, slender pine and right foreground fir follow their painted contours. Lake contact is sloped to follow the actual shore. | The original is deliberately nonphysical in its depth and shadow shapes. Near trees use painted cutouts. Rock backs and scene continuations use restrained samples of the painting's pigments. |
| Through the canyon / 6345 | Wide symmetric passage between repeated isolated rocks, unlike the enormous plum and peach walls and narrow slot. | Individually traced cliff masses, distant slot, scree banks, sandstone slab and tiny travelers. Original clouds and vertical paint streaks remain. Cliff geometry extends beyond the image boundaries, and the route passes between the painted talus banks. | The slot's unseen course is not invented into a large extra canyon. The small figures are static painted forms. Cliff thickness and distances remain estimates. |
| Orchard garden / 2667 | Small generic cottage, detached trees, level generic lawn and an unreferenced red barn. | Large right-hand gray gable, white-framed window, sloping green lawn, overhanging apple branches, hedge, six cloth groups, posts, stump, two hens and foreground plants are separate subjects. Original leaf gaps and cloth colors remain. The garden route avoids the hens and follows the reconstructed slope. | Foliage and laundry use two-sided cutouts. Hidden house surfaces and areas behind branches use paint-based extrapolation. The source does not show an open interior; the earlier unrelated barn and interior inventory are removed. |
| Breaking waves / 0786 | Side-facing harbor, sailboats, forest and boathouse absent from the source; no faithful diagonal surf. | Straight distant sea horizon, low left headland, separate breakers, individual dark reef masses, near foam edge and sand. Actual thick white paint and canvas weave are retained. Walking is confined by the shore-water boundary. | Surf is sculptural and static, preserving the painted wave positions. It is not a fluid simulation. The old harbor route and boathouse are removed. |
| Main street / 0169 | Centered road with symmetrical repeated houses, rather than a close one-sided sidewalk. | Receding right-hand facade plane, cropped red canopy, separate red and dark projecting signs, foreground sandwich boards, tree trunk/crown, bin, planter and sidewalk. Sign depths were corrected so they appear in front of the wall. | Signs preserve the marks in the painting; no shop names are guessed. The street's perspective is estimated, and plant crowns remain painted cutouts. The unseen city and unrelated cafe interior are not manufactured. |

The garden remains an explicitly designed navigation space rather than a reconstruction of a seventh source scene. It continues to contain all 61 original artworks and six painting portals. Photographic margins remain available in the full original collection; the aligned comparison uses only the canvas area.

The principal tradeoff is deliberate: recognizable composition and actual painted marks take precedence over a large generic game map. The scene camera remains freely navigable, but inferred backs and cutout construction become more apparent at large angles. Neither pixel alignment nor the use of a source projection establishes true metric depth from a single painting.
